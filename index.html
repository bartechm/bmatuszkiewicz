THREE CAMPAIGNS AND THEIR ORDER

From the simplest campaign to the most complex

Campaign 1 · Private seller Promotion before the listing expires 7 days before the listing ends: a price tip or a promotion offer. Campaign 2 · Dealer Renew and promote what is in demand One message before listings expire, naming the cars the market wants. Campaign 3 · Buyer Digest with a near-miss We add one listing that almost matches to the saved-search digest.

Order

  • The campaigns run from simplest to most complex: 1 is a quick win on simple data (expiry date, price), 2 needs demand data, 3 changes the digest content and needs a series of experiments.
  • All three are independent of each other, so launch order depends on team capacity. I recommend starting with 1.
  • Direct revenue from promotions comes from campaigns 1 and 2. Campaign 3 measures buyer engagement, and its monetary value is inferred, not measured.

Can we measure incrementality

CampaignRandomisation unitMeasurabilityMain reason

3UserHighLargest sample and clean randomisation, but on our base we can only detect a lift from about 5.5% (calculation in Part 2).

Shared rules: we randomise after entry into the campaign, at the level where the decision is made, and the assignment stays fixed until the test ends. The analysis covers everyone assigned, and the decision rule is written down before launch. Each campaign has its own control group and measures something different, so they work independently.

CAMPAIGN 1 · PRIVATE SELLER

A seller whose listing expires in 7 days gets a price tip or a promotion offer

Seven days before a private listing expires, the seller gets one message. If the price is clearly above the median of similar listings, the message is about price. Otherwise it offers a promotion for the final days.

Why
Before expiry the seller decides anyway whether to give the listing another chance. A private seller usually does not know whether the price is right, so we talk about price first and sell the promotion second. Promoting an overpriced car does not work.
Frequency
One message per listing. If the listing is renewed, the next one comes only 7 days before the next expiry.
Price path
Price clearly above the median of similar listings (the threshold for "clearly" is a hypothesis for the first test). The message suggests adjusting the price and does not offer a promotion.
Goal
Promotion purchase within 7 days of assignment (the message is sent on the assignment day), incremental versus a group with no message.
Channels
Email is the core. An in-app message when the seller opens "My listings" is a supplement. A push the day before expiry is a separate test.

Pick the listing's price to see the message

The message, amounts and numbers are illustrative. The size of the suggested price change (e.g. 5–10%) is a hypothesis to test. The tip is calculated against the median of similar listings and limited to one cut at a time.

    CAMPAIGN 1 · DELIVERY AND RISKS

    What has to be delivered, what can go wrong and how we measure

    Delivery

    What has to happen for the campaign to launch.

    • The expiry date of private listings or, if there is none, the publication date (a question for the team).
    • Listing price versus the median of similar listings (same model, year, mileage), calculated daily by Otomoto and passed to Braze as an attribute. Braze calculates nothing.
    • Listing statuses (sold, deleted, renewed, promoted) for the exit conditions.
    • A promotion purchase event from Otomoto to Braze, to measure incrementality.
    • Legal review of price and promotion messaging and of consents: a promotion offer is a commercial message, not a transactional one.
    Risks

    And how we limit them.

    RiskMitigation
    Promoting a car with an inflated priceWhen the price is clearly above the median, the message is about price and offers no promotion.
    Car sold but listing not closedThe text includes "Sold? End your listing" next to the offer. A status change takes the seller out of the flow.
    Median inflated by listings that just sit thereWe compare with similar listings and say "in similar listings", not "market price".
    Price tip seen as steering the sellerNeutral wording ("consider"), the median shown, one cut at a time and legal review.
    Some sellers would buy a promotion anywayA random control group. Incrementality is measured above what they would have bought without the message.
    Metrics

    How we will know it works.

    Primary
    Share of listings with a promotion purchase within 7 days of assignment (promotion path), incremental versus the group with no message.
    Secondary
    Price change after the message (price path), contacts per listing, renewals, return to the dashboard.
    Guardrails
    Unsubscribes, complaints, listings deleted without a sale (separately from sold ones).
    Channel tests
    Push the day before expiry for those who did not open the email, versus email alone. Outside the campaign core.
    Measuring incrementality

    Can we honestly separate the campaign's effect from what would have happened anyway.

    Randomisation
    At listing level, on the day the listing reaches the expiry threshold, before we check the price. Assignment is permanent.
    Control group
    Listings with no message. We compare the price path and the promotion path separately.
    Analysis
    All assigned listings, not only those that opened the message.
    What could distort it
    The baseline promotion purchase rate is an assumption to be measured (we assume about 3%). At that rate, a 1 percentage point lift is detectable with about 5k listings per group and half a point with about 20k. With about 6k listings entering per week (model assumptions) that is 2 weeks of testing for 1 point and about 6 weeks for half a point. Listings that are sold but not closed lower the rate equally in both groups.
    Assessment
    Measurability high to medium. Clean randomisation at a single moment and a metric that equals revenue.
    CAMPAIGN 2 · DEALER

    Renew and promote what is in demand

    As a dealer's listings approach expiry, they get one message listing the cars the market currently wants most, together with a "renew and promote" package offer.

    Hypothesis
    A dealer does not know which of their cars are most in demand, but Otomoto does. A promotion shown at the renewal decision, backed by evidence of demand, persuades more than a generic offer.
    How it differs from campaign 1
    The trigger is the same but the situation is different. A private seller has one car and does not know whether the price is right, so we help judge the price. A dealer has many cars, knows their prices, does not know demand and can promote only some of them, so we help choose which.
    Choosing cars
    High demand relative to the number of competing listings (not just model popularity), a price not clearly above the median (the same indicator as campaign 1, we do not promote such listings) and low current exposure.
    Frequency
    One message per dealer, not per car. At most one per week. The trigger is "X days to expiry", and X is a hypothesis for the first test.
    Channels
    Email and a message in the dealer panel. SMS only as a narrow test for high-demand listings expiring within 48 hours.

      CAMPAIGN 2 · DELIVERY AND RISKS

      What has to be delivered, what can go wrong and how we measure

      Delivery

      What has to happen for the campaign to launch.

      • Daily demand data at model and segment level (searches, watched, contacts) with the number of competing listings. Otomoto calculates the indicator and passes it to the Braze catalog as a column.
      • Expiry dates of dealer listings and a price indicator versus the median of similar listings (the same one as in campaign 1).
      • Which dealers have auto-renewal or a promotion included in their plan. This is the key question for the team: without it the message reaches people for whom expiry is not a decision moment.
      • Context: AutoIQ and listing performance reports already reach dealers today, so the message does not promise analytics, it points to specific cars.
      Risks

      And how we limit them.

      RiskMitigation
      Dealer buys a promotion for a car that would have sold anywayA dealer-level control group. Incrementality is measured above what they would have bought without the message.
      Dealer buys a promotion for a car that will not sell because of its priceWe exclude listings with a price clearly above the median.
      Send moment too late or too earlyFirst test: how many days before expiry (X).
      Too many messages to large accountsOne message per dealer per week, and for large accounts an alert to the account manager.
      Metrics

      How we will know it works.

      Primary
      Share of dealers with at least one incremental promotion purchase within 7 days from the moment the dealer qualifies (gets the message, or in the control group would have got it), incremental versus the group with no message.
      Secondary
      Renewals (counted separately from promotions), dashboard visits, contacts per promoted car.
      Guardrails
      Unsubscribes, messages per dealer, churn from the platform.
      Channel tests
      SMS for high-demand listings expiring within 48 hours versus email alone. Outside the campaign core.
      Measuring incrementality

      Can we honestly separate the campaign's effect from what would have happened anyway.

      Randomisation
      At dealer level, stratified by account size and plan, so that a few large dealers do not dominate the result.
      Groups
      Three arms: no message, the "renew and promote" offer alone, and the offer with evidence of demand for specific cars. Without the third arm we cannot separate the effect of the demand information from the offer itself.
      What could distort it
      We do not know the number of dealers (a question for the team). With few dealers and big differences between them the result is noisy: measuring many expiring listings per dealer and a longer window help. Decisions are made by a few people in each company. One dealer's promotion pushes other offers down, so on the buyer side it is a zero-sum game.
      Assessment
      Measurability low to medium. The smallest population and the greatest variability. We assume we can detect only a large effect.
      CAMPAIGN 3 · BUYER

      We add one listing that almost matches to the saved search

      In the daily digest, below the usual results, the user sees one listing that meets everything except one condition. It is clearly labelled with how and by how much it differs.

      Why
      A saved search is a declared intent. Sharp filters hide listings a person would consider, e.g. 108,000 km against a limit of 100,000. The digest is already sent, so the campaign changes its content and does not create a new channel.
      Matching
      The near-miss is built from the user's own filters: hard ones (e.g. condition, make, model, gearbox) stay unchanged, and one soft filter (mileage, price, distance, year) is relaxed within a threshold set separately for each.
      Engagement
      Contacts with a seller (phone, chat) per user per week, incremental versus the group with no near-miss.

        CAMPAIGN 3 · DELIVERY AND RISKS

        What has to be delivered, what can go wrong and how we measure

        Delivery

        What has to happen for the campaign to launch.

        • A listings catalog in Braze updated via API, and the user's saved search as flat attributes (one main search, with a search ID). A selection is limited to 10 filters, so we use the most important hard filters.
        • A "contact" event (phone reveal, chat message) from Otomoto to Braze, to measure incrementality.
        • The "See more listings like this" link is today's digest link with one range parameter raised (e.g. mileage up to 115,000 instead of 100,000). It needs no changes on the results page.
        • Monitoring the share of empty selections: a selection with a disallowed Liquid tag returns zero listings without an error.
        • A user with several saved searches gets a near-miss for only one of them: the one they open most often.
        • The "has near-miss" flag (a listing within the threshold exists) is calculated daily by Otomoto for every recipient, holdout included, independently of the Braze flow. It is needed before randomisation.
        Risks

        And how we limit them.

        RiskMitigation
        Cannibalisation: the near-miss takes contacts from the regular listingsWe measure total contacts for everyone assigned, so cannibalisation counts itself. Contacts from exact matches are tracked as well.
        Near-miss seen as noiseThe "Don't show near matches" link and its click rate as a guardrail. Only one filter at a time, with a threshold set separately for each filter.
        Novelty effect inflates the first weekWe report the first week separately and base the decision on the second. The test runs 2–4 weeks.
        Metrics

        How we will know it works.

        Primary
        Share of users with at least one seller contact in the second week after assignment (days 8–14), incremental versus the holdout.
        Secondary
        Clicks on the near-miss block, listing views from the digest.
        Guardrails
        Contacts from exact matches, unsubscribes, "don't show" clicks, deleted saved searches.
        Measuring incrementality

        Can we honestly separate the campaign's effect from what would have happened anyway.

        Randomisation
        At user level, after entry into the campaign and before the listing is shown, among people with a near-miss available. Assignment is permanent.
        Control group
        A holdout with the regular digest, no near-miss.
        Analysis
        Everyone assigned, not only those who clicked. That way we do not have to attribute contacts to the campaign, and cannibalisation is inside the result.
        What could distort it
        A contact is not a sale: we measure buyer activity, not cars sold. An extra contact with one listing may take it from another, so the total counts, not a single listing.
        Assessment
        Measurability high. The largest sample of the three campaigns and the cleanest randomisation, although on our base we can detect a lift only from about 5.5%.
        PART 2 · CAMPAIGN 3

        The message and the experiments: what we send, what we test first and how we know it works

        1 Message→ 2 Experiment 1→ 3 Experiment 2→ 4 Experiment 3→ 5 Further queue
        1 · Message

        What exactly the user receives.

        When
        In the daily digest, at a fixed time.
        Where
        The core is email from saved searches, one block below the usual listings. In addition an in-app card, tested separately (test queue).
        What
        One near-miss, clearly marked. Below it a "See more listings like this" link, which opens the regular results page of the same search with one parameter relaxed (as in today's digest links), and "Don't show near matches".
        How it sounds
        Don't miss out: meets all your criteria except mileage (108,000 km instead of 100,000)
        Which filter, when there are several options
        Only one filter at a time. The order of checking is fixed in advance (hypothesis to test: mileage, price, distance, year). Braze checks the selections in turn and takes the first one that returns a listing within the threshold. Starting thresholds (hypotheses to test): mileage up to +15%, price up to +10%, distance up to +20 km, year up to −1. Which filter works best is decided by a test from the queue.
        Which listing, when there are several
        Among listings added since the last digest that fall within the threshold, we show the one closest to what the user asked for. For mileage that is the lowest mileage above the limit, for price the lowest price above the limit, for year the newest year below the limit. This is a plain sort in the selection by the relaxed filter's column. It does not work for distance, because a selection does not calculate distance from the user, so there we take the newest. Limiting to new listings means the same one does not come back every day. In experiment 2 the same sort works separately among promoted and non-promoted listings.
        2 · Experiment 1

        Does a near-miss in the email increase contacts.

        Design
        Two equal groups among people for whom Otomoto calculated that a near-miss is available: holdout (regular digest) and near-miss (digest with the block, without the "Promoted" label). We randomise after entry into the campaign, before the listing is shown. Assignment is fixed until the test ends.
        Why first
        Without an effect of the near-miss in email, the other tests (promoted, page, which filter) make no sense. Email is also the cheapest test: it needs no changes on the Otomoto side.
        Primary metric
        Share of users with at least one contact in the second week after assignment (days 8–14). We report the first week separately, because the novelty effect inflates it.
        Guardrails
        Contacts from exact matches (cannibalisation), unsubscribes, "don't show" clicks, deleted saved searches.
        Hypothesis
        A near-miss in email increases the share of users who contact a seller, and does not take contacts from exact matches.
        Decision (written before launch)
        Contacts increase, and unsubscribes and "don't show" clicks stay below a threshold set before launch: we roll out to everyone and move to experiments 2 and 3. No increase, or the threshold is exceeded: we stop in this form.
        Sample

        We size the test so that it rarely shows an effect that is not there and detects a real one in 4 cases out of 5. The baseline contact rate and the share of people with a near-miss are assumptions that the first measurement will replace with real numbers.

        Needed per group
        Available per group (from 338k recipients)
        Smallest detectable relative lift

        People enter the test from their first digest, so most of the base is in it from the start. When the available sample is smaller than needed, we extend the test (new saved searches keep arriving) or accept that a smaller effect will not be detected. The novelty effect inflates the first days, so we report results week by week.

        3 · Experiment 2

        Does a near-miss with a promoted listing work as well as a non-promoted one.

        Design
        Among people with a near-miss for whom Otomoto also has a promoted listing within the threshold (a flag calculated by Otomoto), we randomise half and half: a promoted near-miss (marked "Promoted") or a non-promoted one. Everyone else gets the non-promoted one.
        Why
        Promoted listings are extra value for sellers and for Otomoto, as long as buyers react to them as they do to regular ones.
        Primary metric
        The same as in experiment 1.
        Hypothesis
        The promoted near-miss is not worse than the non-promoted one by more than a margin set before launch (proposal: 10% relative).
        Decision (written before launch)
        Not worse: we show the promoted one where there is one, and the non-promoted one where there is not. Worse: we stay with the non-promoted one.
        Sample
        Assumption: about 30% of people with a near-miss have a promoted listing, i.e. about 25k people per group under the baseline assumptions. That lets us detect a difference of about 10% relative, so the 10% margin is what this test actually settles.
        Caveat
        The test covers listings that are already promoted, so it says nothing about listings that are not.
        4 · Experiment 3

        Does the same block on the page after the click add contacts beyond the email alone.

        What we change
        After a click on "See all new listings", the same near-miss with the same label appears at the end of the results list (view in the mock-up above).
        Start condition
        Experiment 1 showed an effect in email, experiment 2 is finished (one test at a time on the same segment), and Otomoto confirms that the results page can show the block to only some users, i.e. knows their group. If not, we run another test from the queue instead.
        Design
        Among people with a near-miss in the email we randomise half and half: the block on the page or no block. The email is identical in both arms, so comparing those who clicked is fair. The link carries the near-miss ID and the group.
        Primary metric
        Share of clickers with a contact within 7 days of the click.
        Guardrails
        Contacts from exact matches, scroll depth, unsubscribes.
        Sample
        Assumption: about 170k people with a near-miss in the email (after rollout to everyone eligible), 15% click rate, 15% baseline contact rate among clickers. That gives about 13k clickers per arm, enough to detect a lift from about 8.5% relative.
        Caveat
        This step goes beyond Braze itself, so in the brief it is an extension, not the core. To be confirmed with Otomoto: whether the results page allows such a block and whether it is their work.
